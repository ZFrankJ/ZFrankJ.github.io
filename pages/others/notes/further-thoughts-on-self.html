关于我相的进一步思考以及指导意义

← Back to Notes

Journal entry — Thursday, May 28, 2026

人并不是凭空选择自己的人。所谓“我”的判断、偏好、意志与价值观，都是先天禀赋、身体状态、社会环境、语言结构与后天经历长期塑造后的结果。人脑并不直接拥有完整的世界，而是在不断抽象、压缩和解释输入；而所有压缩都必然有损，因此每个人看到的世界都带有自身历史的痕迹。我们之所以能沟通，是因为社会、语言和共同经验塑造出了一部分共享的抽象空间；而我们之所以彼此不同，则是因为在快乐、意义、尊严、恐惧、爱憎和人生目标这些更高层的概念上，每个人都承载着不同的压缩方式。

因此，理解一个人，不应只是判断他说了什么，而应尝试理解他为何会这样说。他的说辞并不必然正确，却一定有其生成路径；我的判断也并不天然高于他人，而只是我在当前信息、经验和模型下给出的结果。尊重他人，不意味着接受一切观点，而是尊重每一种观点背后的形成过程；坚持判断，也不意味着执着于自我，而是要在检验现实的同时保留修正自己的可能。

真正成熟的思考，是在“我相”升起之后仍能看见它：看见自己的情绪如何影响判断，看见自己的经验如何伪装成普遍规律，看见自己的立场如何筛选证据。跳脱出“我”，并不是否定自我，而是把自我也纳入观察对象；不是放弃行动，而是在行动前多一层觉察。这样的人不会轻易轻蔑他人，也不会轻易神化自己；不会因为理解因果而否定责任，也不会因为承认塑造而放弃成长。

所谓自由，或许并不是脱离因果的绝对选择，而是在看见自身如何被塑造之后，仍然拥有重新塑造自己的能力。人无法完全跳出过去，但可以重新解释过去；人无法凭空成为另一个人，但可以通过反思、训练和环境选择，逐渐改变自己的默认反应。认识到“我”是被形成的，不是为了消解自己，而是为了不再被旧的自己完全支配。真正的清醒，也许就是不再把自己的压缩模型误认为世界本身。

既然我看见了我的模型，我就不必永远被它支配。

Journal entry — Thursday, May 28, 2026

No one chooses who they are in a vacuum. What we call the “self”—our judgments, preferences, will, and values—is the product of long-term shaping by innate dispositions, physical condition, social environment, the structures of language, and accumulated experience. The human brain does not hold a complete representation of the world directly; instead, it continually abstracts, compresses, and interprets what it receives. All compression is necessarily lossy, so the world each of us sees bears the traces of our own history. We are able to communicate because society, language, and shared experience have created a partly shared space of abstraction. We differ because, when it comes to higher-level concepts such as happiness, meaning, dignity, fear, love and hatred, and the aims of life, each of us encodes them through a different pattern of compression.

To understand someone, then, we should not merely judge what they say, but try to understand why they say it. Their account is not necessarily correct, but it always has a path by which it came to be. Nor is my own judgment inherently superior to anyone else’s; it is simply the conclusion produced by the information, experience, and model currently available to me. Respecting others does not mean accepting every view. It means recognizing and respecting the process by which each view took shape. Maintaining one’s own judgment does not mean clinging to the self; it means testing that judgment against reality while remaining willing to revise oneself.

Truly mature thought is able to recognize the “notion of self” even after it arises: to see how one’s emotions influence judgment, how one’s experience disguises itself as universal law, and how one’s standpoint filters the evidence. Stepping outside the “self” does not mean denying it, but making the self another object of observation. It does not mean giving up action, but adding another layer of awareness before acting. A person who can do this will not readily look down on others or exalt themselves. They will not deny responsibility because they understand causality, nor abandon growth because they recognize how deeply people are shaped.

Perhaps freedom is not an absolute power of choice outside cause and effect, but the ability to reshape ourselves after seeing how we have been shaped. We cannot step entirely outside our past, but we can reinterpret it. We cannot become someone else from nothing, but through reflection, training, and the environments we choose, we can gradually alter our default responses. Recognizing that the “self” was formed is not an attempt to dissolve it, but a way of no longer being completely ruled by who we once were. True lucidity may simply be the refusal to mistake our compressed model of the world for the world itself.

Once I can see my model, I need not remain ruled by it forever.
